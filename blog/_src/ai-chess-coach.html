Chess engines have been stronger than every human for decades, but they've always been bad teachers. "+2.4" doesn't tell you what you were thinking wrong. An AI chess coach tries to close that gap by putting the engine's verdict into words.

What a good AI coach does

  • Finds the turning point. Out of 40 moves, which one or two decided the game?
  • Explains in human terms. "Your bishop was defending f7; moving it let the knight in" beats "Bd3?? −3.1".
  • Stays grounded in the engine. Language models alone are bad at chess and will happily invent moves. A good coach checks every claim against a real engine.
  • Remembers your history. One review helps; seeing the same weakness across 20 games is what changes how you play.
  • Points you at practice. A weakness should come with a drill.

Where it falls short

  • Accountability. A human coach notices when you stop doing the work. An app can remind you, but it's easy to ignore.
  • Psychology and tournament prep. Nerves, time management in a long event, preparing for a specific opponent: humans are still better here.
  • Occasional wrong explanations. Even engine-grounded explanations can oversimplify. If something sounds off, play the line out on the board.

How to choose one

  1. Does it run a real engine? Check that the best moves are actually best (paste one position into Lichess).
  2. Is the review instant and unlimited, or metered? You want to review every game, not ration reviews.
  3. Does it explain, or just label? "Blunder" isn't coaching.
  4. Can you try it free? A week of real games will tell you whether the explanations help.

FAQ

Can AI replace a chess coach?

For explaining your games and finding your weaknesses, it covers a lot of what a coach does at a fraction of the cost. For accountability, psychology and serious tournament prep, a human is still better.

Can ChatGPT coach me at chess?

General chatbots are unreliable at chess on their own: they misread positions and suggest illegal moves. Use a coach that checks its explanations against a real engine.
