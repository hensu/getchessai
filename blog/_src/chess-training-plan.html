You have a job, maybe kids, and half an hour a day for chess. You don't need a 40-book reading list. You need a plan simple enough to keep doing for three months.

The principle: play, review, fix

Under about 1600, almost everyone improves fastest with the same loop: play games slow enough to think, find out what went wrong, and practise exactly that. Opening theory and strategy books can wait. Most games at this level are decided by tactics and blunders, and that's where time pays back first.

The weekly plan (30 minutes a day)

DayWhat to do
MonOne rapid game (10+5 or slower), then review it: find the biggest mistake for each side.
Tue15 minutes of puzzles on your weakest theme, then one rapid game.
WedOne rapid game and review.
ThuEndgame practice: king and pawn endings, or one basic checkmate you can't yet do with your eyes closed.
FriOne rapid game and review.
SatLonger session: two or three games, review all of them.
SunRest, or look back at the week's mistakes and write down your top one.

With an hour a day, add a second game on review days and double the puzzle time. Don't add more topics.

How to pick your "weakest theme"

Every review, tag the losing mistake with one word: hung piece, fork, pin, back rank, endgame, time. After two weeks, count. Your top tag is your theme for the next two weeks of puzzles. When it stops showing up, move to the next one.

Keep the toolset small. One place to play, one place to review, one set of puzzles. Switching tools feels like progress but isn't.

What not to do (yet)

  • Memorising opening lines. Learn the ideas of one opening as White and one defence to each of 1.e4 and 1.d4. That's enough until 1500 or so.
  • Bullet. Fun, but you can't practise checking your moves at one minute.
  • Endless puzzle rush. Speed puzzles train pattern recognition, but slow, careful puzzles on your weak theme train what's costing you games.

How to tell if it's working

Ratings are noisy week to week. Instead track two numbers every two weeks:

  1. Blunders per game (moves that lose a piece or more). This should fall first.
  2. Your rating over a month. Expect it to wobble; the trend is what counts.

If blunders aren't falling after a month, slow down: longer time controls, and the 10-second safety check on every move.

FAQ

How many games a week should I play?

Five to ten slow games you actually review beat thirty you don't.

Do I need a coach?

A human coach is great if you can afford one. Without one, the key thing a coach does is tell you what you keep getting wrong, which you can get from reviewing every game honestly, or from an AI coach that reviews them for you.
