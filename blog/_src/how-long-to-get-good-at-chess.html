Months to beat your friends, a year or two to be a strong club player, and many years to reach expert. The range is wide because the biggest factor isn't talent, it's how you spend your chess time.

Rough timelines for adult beginners

These assume you play and study most days and review your games. Playing casually without studying is much slower, and many people never pass their first plateau that way.

Every number here is a range, not a promise. Some people move much faster; plenty of people stay at one level for years.

What makes the difference

  • Reviewing games. The single biggest gap between people who improve and people who don't. Playing without reviewing repeats the same mistakes.
  • Slower time controls. You learn in rapid and classical; you mostly perform what you already know in blitz and bullet.
  • Targeted puzzles. Practising the tactic you keep missing beats random puzzles.
  • Consistency. 30 minutes a day beats a five-hour weekend binge.
  • Opponents near your level. You learn most from games you could have won.

Why most people plateau

The common plateaus sit around 800–1000 and 1200–1400 online. Both usually have the same cause: playing lots of fast games, reviewing none, and blundering the same way over and over. The way out is boring: slow down, cut blunders, and follow a simple training plan.

FAQ

Am I too old to get good at chess?

No. Adults improve more slowly than children at the very top end, but reaching a strong club level as an adult is common, especially if you study deliberately.

How many hours a day should I practise?

Half an hour to an hour of focused practice a day is plenty for steady progress. Quality beats quantity.

What does "good at chess" mean?

For most people it means beating friends and family and being above the average on the site they play on. See what counts as a good chess rating.
