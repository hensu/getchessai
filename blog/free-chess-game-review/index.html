Free chess game review: how to review every game without paying

7 min read

You just lost a game you thought you were winning. You hit "Game Review" and get told you've used your free review for the day. Here's how to review every game for free, and how to get more out of a review than a list of red question marks.

Why reviewing every game matters

Most club-level games aren't decided by opening theory or deep strategy. They're decided by one or two moments: a piece left hanging, a fork you didn't see, a pawn push that opened your king. If you only review the games you have credits for, you miss the pattern. The same mistake usually shows up in game after game, and you only notice it when you look at all of them.

How to review a game in five minutes

An engine review is only useful if you do something with it. This routine works whatever tool you use:

  1. Before you open the engine, name the moment you think you lost. Your guess is the thing you're testing. If you're often wrong, that itself is a lesson: you don't know when you're in danger.
  2. Find the biggest swing in the evaluation graph. One drop usually matters more than ten small inaccuracies. Ignore anything under about half a pawn.
  3. Ask "what did I miss?", not "what was the best move?". Was it a capture you didn't see? A check? A piece you forgot was attacked? Put it in one sentence.
  4. Play the better move out on the board. Seeing the line beats reading it. (Chess AI's paid plan lets you retry the position against the engine until you find it.)
  5. Write down the category. Hanging piece, missed fork, back-rank, bad trade. After ten games you'll have a short list of what's actually costing you rating.

Rule of thumb: if the same category shows up three times in a week, stop playing for a day and do puzzles on that theme only.

What an engine can't tell you

Engines are perfect at what and silent on why. "+3.2 after 18…Nxe4" doesn't explain that you missed it because you were looking at your own attack and never checked your opponent's captures. That gap is where a coach helps: turning a list of bad moves into one or two habits to fix. It's also why Chess AI's paid coaching writes the review in plain language and tells you what to practise next.

FAQ

Is Chess.com Game Review free?

Partly. Free members get a limited number of full Game Reviews per day; unlimited reviews are part of Chess.com's paid memberships. The basic analysis board is free.

Is Lichess analysis really unlimited?

Yes. Lichess is free and open source, and its computer analysis has no daily cap.

Can I review games I played on Chess.com somewhere else?

Yes. Download or copy the game's PGN and paste it into any analysis board that accepts PGN.
